# Chapter 8 Outline: Cross-Correlations and Future Prospects

## 1. Introduction: The Need for New Probes
*Goal: Explain why we need to move beyond single-source detection (Parts II & III) and into large-scale statistics (Part IV).*

### 1.1 Limitations of Individual Source Searches
- **Narrative:** We have pushed point-source detection to its limit (Chapters 5-7). The remaining dark matter signal is likely diffuse and buried in noise.
- **Key Concepts:**
    - The "Confusion Limit": When sources overlap, individual detection becomes impossible.
    - The Background Problem: Distinguishing diffuse DM from diffuse astrophysical backgrounds (IGRB) is degenerate if we only look at the energy spectrum.
- **Transition:** We need a *spatial* handle to separate DM from astrophysics.

### 1.2 The Angular Power Spectrum (APS)
- **Narrative:** Introduce the statistical tool of choice. Instead of counting sources, we measure the "clumpiness" of the sky at different angular scales.
- **Key Concepts:**
    - **Multipole Expansion:** $C_\ell$ spectrum.
    - **1-halo vs 2-halo terms:** probing DM substructure ($1h$, small scales) vs. cosmic web distribution ($2h$, large scales).
- **Key References:**
    - *Fornengo & Regis (2014)* (Review of APS formalism)

## 2. Theoretical Framework: Cross-Correlations
*Goal: Explain why correlating lambda-CDM tracers (Galaxies) with Gamma-rays is the "Golden Channel".*

### 2.1 The Concept of Tomography
- **Narrative:** Seeing the Universe in slices.
- **Key Concepts:**
    - **Window Functions ($W(z)$):** The probability of seeing a signal from redshift $z$.
    - **Matching Windows:** If DM is at $z=0.1$, we should look for galaxies at $z=0.1$.
    - **Noise Rejection:** An astrophysical background at $z=1.0$ (like Blazars) will *not* correlate with a galaxy catalog at $z=0.1$, effectively vanishing from the cross-correlation signal.
- **Key References:**
    - *Camera et al. (2013)* (Cross-correlations with Shear/Galaxies)

### 2.2 The "Golden Channel": Low-Redshift Galaxies
- **Narrative:** Where is the DM signal strongest? Nearby.
- **Key Concepts:**
    - **DM Annihilation:** Scales as $\rho^2$. The signal is dominated by the nearest structures (Local Universe, $z < 0.3$).
    - **Astrophysical Backgrounds:** Blazars and Star-Forming Galaxies peak at higher redshifts ($z \sim 1$).
    - **Conclusion:** Correlating with *local* catalogs (2MASS, 2MPZ) maximizes Signal-to-Noise.

## 3. The Future: The Cherenkov Telescope Array Observatory (CTAO)
*Goal: Introduce the instrument that will make this possible.*

### 3.1 From *Fermi*-LAT to CTAO
- **Narrative:** *Fermi* has done amazing work, but it is small. CTAO is the future.
- **Key Upgrades:**
    - **Collection Area:** $~10^5 \times$ larger than *Fermi* (better statistics for short timescales).
    - **Angular Resolution:** Crucial for resolving small-scale anisotropies.
    - **Energy Range:** Pushing to TeV scales where heavy WIMPs might live.

### 3.2 The Extragalactic Survey (EGAL)
- **Narrative:** How will CTAO observe the sky?
- **Key Concepts:**
    - The planned wide-field survey of the extragalactic sky.
    - Uniform sensitivity requirements essential for anisotropy studies.

## 4. Forecasting Discovery Reach (Paper 5)
*Goal: Present the results of the specific study in this thesis (Pinetti et al. 2025).*

### 4.1 Auto-Correlation vs. Cross-Correlation
- **Narrative:** Comparing the two methods for CTAO.
- **Key Findings:**
    - **Auto-correlation:** Limited by the "shot noise" of bright blazars. If we don't mask them perfectly, they ruin the DM search.
    - **Cross-correlation:** Robust against blazar contamination.
    - **Result:** Cross-correlation yields a factor of $\sim 5-10$ improvement in sensitivity over auto-correlation.

### 4.2 Sensitivity to Dark Matter
- **Narrative:** What can we actually discover?
- **Key Results:**
    - We forecast sensitivity to Thermal WIMP cross-sections ($\langle \sigma v \rangle \sim 3 \times 10^{-26} cm^3/s$) for heavy masses (> 1 TeV).
    - This complements the *Fermi*-LAT constraints (which are best at low masses) and Dwarf Spheroidal searches.

## 5. Conclusion
- **Summary:** We demonstrated that Cross-Correlations with low-redshift galaxy catalogs are the most promising avenue for Dark Matter detection with CTAO.
- **Specific Findings:**
    - The "Golden Channel" of $z < 0.1$ minimizes astrophysical backgrounds while maximizing the DM signal.
    - This method provides a robust, complementary probe to traditional methods (like Dwarf Spheroidals), offering a unique handle on the particle nature of Dark Matter through Large Scale Structure.
