# Chapter 5: Searching for Dark Matter Substructures - References & Sources

## 1. Reviews & Textbooks
*General consensus, theoretical foundations, and state-of-the-art summaries.*

### J. S. Bullock and M. Boylan-Kolchin (2017) - "Small-Scale Challenges to the $\Lambda$CDM Paradigm"
*   **Source:** **arXiv:1707.04256**
    *   **Relevance:** This review provides a comprehensive overview of the discrepancies between $\Lambda$CDM predictions and observations at the scale of dwarf galaxies. It specifically addresses the "missing satellite" problem and the "too big to fail" problem, which are the primary motivations for searching for dark matter subhalos.

### C. S. Frenk and S. D. M. White (2012) - "Dark matter and cosmic structure"
*   **Source:** **arXiv:1210.0544**
    *   **Relevance:** This paper reviews the successes of the Cold Dark Matter model in reproducing large-scale structure while highlighting the hierarchical nature of structure formation. It discusses how dark matter halos form and evolve, providing context for the distribution of substructure.

### J. Zavala and C. S. Frenk (2019) - "Dark Matter Haloes and Subhaloes"
*   **Source:** **arXiv:1907.11775**
    *   **Relevance:** A modern update to earlier reviews, focusing on the internal structure and abundance of dark matter halos and subhalos as predicted by simulations. It serves as a key reference for the theoretical properties of the targets sought in this chapter.

### M. Fornasa and M. A. Sánchez-Conde (2015) - "The nature of the Diffuse Gamma-Ray Background"
*   **Source:** **arXiv:1502.02866**
    *   **Relevance:** Unlike the structural reviews, this focuses on the gamma-ray signature. It analyzes the composition of the Diffuse Gamma-Ray Background (DGRB) and specifically addresses the potential for dark matter annihilation or decay to contribute to the isotropic emission.

## 2. Key Specific Papers
*Primary sources for specific claims, historical limits, and experimental results.*

*   **W. H. Press and P. Schechter (1974):** *Formation of galaxies and clusters of galaxies by self-similar gravitational condensation*
    *   **Source:** *ApJ, 187, 425 (1974)* [Foundational, no arXiv]
    *   **Relevance:** Established the analytical formalism for predicting the number density of halos as a function of mass (the halo mass function). It provides the theoretical foundation for understanding how hierarchical clustering leads to the abundance of structures.

*   **B. Moore et al. (1999):** *Dark matter substructure within galactic halos*
    *   **Source:** *astro-ph/9907411*
    *   **Relevance:** The seminal paper identifying the "missing satellite problem" by comparing N-body simulations to the observed population of dwarf galaxies. It highlighted that $\Lambda$CDM predictions yield far more dark matter substructures (dark satellites) than observed.

*   **V. Springel et al. (2008):** *The Aquarius Project: the subhalos of galactic halos*
    *   **Source:** *arXiv:0809.0898*
    *   **Relevance:** Presents results from the Aquarius Project, ultra-high-resolution N-body simulations of Milky Way-sized halos. It provides detailed statistics on the subhalo mass function and spatial distribution, confirming the hierarchical nature of $\Lambda$CDM down to low masses.

*   **L. E. Strigari et al. (2008):** *The most dark-matter-dominated galaxies: predicted gamma-ray signals from the faintest Milky Way dwarfs*
    *   **Source:** *Nature 454, 1096*
    *   **Relevance:** Establishes that dSphs are the most dark-matter-dominated systems known. It demonstrates that these galaxies are embedded in massive dark matter halos, making them prime targets for indirect dark matter detection.

### 5.2 The Unassociated Source Problem
**Topics:** Criteria for associating gamma-ray sources, classifying unassociated sources as potential dark subhalos

*   **J. Coronado-Blázquez et al. (2019):**
    *   **Source:** **arXiv:1906.11896**
    *   **Relevance:** Details filtering criteria for dark matter subhalo candidates among unassociated sources, including latitude cuts and variability.
*   **B. Bertoni, D. Hooper, and T. Linden (2015):**
    *   **Source:** **arXiv:1504.02087**
    *   **Relevance:** Analyzes 3FGL unassociated sources to identify potential subhalos by applying specific astrophysical cuts.
*   **F. Massaro et al. (2015):**
    *   **Source:** **arXiv:1503.05556**
    *   **Relevance:** Establishes methodology for associating gamma-ray sources with low-energy counterparts, critical for defining the "unassociated" sample.

#### Additional Sources (5.2)
*   **A. Berlin & D. Hooper (2014)** *[arXiv:1309.0525]* - Constraints on DM annihilation from unassociated Fermi-LAT sources.
*   **N. Mirabal et al. (2016)** *[arXiv:1605.00711]* - Supervised ML to classify 3FGL sources as pulsars vs subhalos.
*   **M. Ackermann et al. (2012)** *[arXiv:1108.1202]* - Statistical approach to recognizing source classes in 1FGL.
*   **H.-S. Zechlin et al. (2012)** *[arXiv:1111.3514]* - Filtering unassociated sources for spectra and variability.
*   **M. R. Buckley & D. Hooper (2010)** *[arXiv:1004.1644]* - Early analysis of 1FGL searching for subhalos.
*   **D. Salvetti et al. (2017)** *[arXiv:1705.09832]* - "3FGLzoo" using neural networks to classify unassociated sources.

### 5.3 Machine Learning Classification
**Topics:** Application of methods to source classification, domain shift challenges

*   **A. Amerio et al. (2025) (Paper 4):**
    *   **Source:** **arXiv:2503.14584**
    *   **Relevance:** Explicitly addresses "dataset shift" using quantification learning to distinguish dark matter subhalos from astrophysical backgrounds.
*   **A. Bhat and D. Malyshev (2021):**
    *   **Source:** **arXiv:2102.07642**
    *   **Relevance:** Applies Random Forest and Neural Networks to probabilistically classify unassociated sources.
*   **Mishra-Sharma et al. (2021):**
    *   **Source:** **arXiv:2110.06931**
    *   **Relevance:** Neural simulation-based inference (NPE/SBI) describing domain shift resilience in Galactic Center analysis.
