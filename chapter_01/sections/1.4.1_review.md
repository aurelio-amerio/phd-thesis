## Important Issues (🟡)

### Issue 1: Missing decay lifetime context

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 2 (decay rate equation)
- **Quote**: "$\tau$ is the dark matter lifetime \cite{Hooper:2024, Pinetti:2021jjs}"
- **Problem**: The text introduces $\tau$ but gives no sense of the relevant scale. Unlike the annihilation cross-section — where the thermal relic benchmark ($2.2 \times 10^{-26}$ cm³/s) was established in Section 1.2.2 — the reader has no anchor for what constitutes a "long" or "short" DM lifetime. Current lower bounds ($\tau \gtrsim 10^{28}$ s from Fermi-LAT) far exceed the age of the universe ($\sim 4.3 \times 10^{17}$ s), and stating this would help the reader calibrate the decay signal strength.
- **Suggested fix**: After the decay rate equation, add one sentence: "Current gamma-ray observations constrain the dark matter lifetime to $\tau \gtrsim 10^{28}$ s \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}, far exceeding the age of the universe — making decay an intrinsically rare process whose detection requires enormous integrated exposures."

### Issue 2: Spectral peak positions not qualified as approximate

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 4
- **Quote**: "peaking at $E_\gamma \sim m_\chi / 20$ in $E^2\, dN/dE$ units" and "peaking at $E_\gamma \sim m_\chi / 3$"
- **Problem**: These peak positions are order-of-magnitude estimates and depend on the dark matter mass. The text correctly uses "$\sim$" notation, but does not clarify that these scale with $m_\chi$ in a log-dependent way (the multiplicity of the hadronization cascade grows logarithmically with energy). A reader could interpret these as fixed ratios.
- **Suggested fix**: Add a brief qualifier, e.g.: "peaking at $E_\gamma \sim m_\chi / 20$ in $E^2\, dN/dE$ representation (the exact position shifts logarithmically with $m_\chi$ due to the mass-dependent multiplicity of the hadronization cascade) \cite{Hooper:2024, Cirelli:2024ssz}."

### Issue 3: The branching ratio equation appears without sufficient context

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 3
- **Quote**: The equation $\frac{dN_\gamma}{dE} = \sum_f \text{BR}_f \, \frac{dN_\gamma^{(f)}}{dE}$
- **Problem**: The branching ratios $\text{BR}_f$ are introduced as weights but without noting that in a model-independent analysis, one typically fixes $\text{BR}_f = 1$ for a single channel and presents results channel by channel. The text says "model-independent analysis" but doesn't explain how this equation is actually used in practice (single-channel benchmarks, not a full sum). This could create confusion when the reader encounters the "$b\bar{b}$ benchmark" language immediately after.
- **Suggested fix**: Add a clarifying sentence after the equation: "In practice, limits are derived for one channel at a time — setting $\text{BR}_f = 1$ for a chosen final state — so each `benchmark channel' maps to a distinctive spectral shape."

## Minor Issues (🟢)

### Issue 4: Slight redundancy with Section 1.3.3

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 1
- **Quote**: "their typical velocities are $v \sim 10^{-3}\, c$"
- **Problem**: This fact was already stated in Section 1.3.1 (direct detection) and 1.3.3 (why indirect detection). It is not wrong to restate it — and the context here is specifically about annihilation kinematics — but a very brief callback ("As noted in Section 1.3, ...") would strengthen cohesion.
- **Suggested fix**: Change to: "Dark matter particles in present-day galactic halos are cold — as established in Section~\ref{sec:search}, their typical velocities are $v \sim 10^{-3}\, c$ — and annihilate or decay essentially at rest."

### Issue 5: Citation density in paragraph 1

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 1
- **Quote**: Three consecutive sentences cite `\cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}`, and the fourth cites `\cite{Cirelli:2024ssz, Pinetti:2021jjs}`
- **Problem**: Four Cirelli citations in four sentences. While each is justified, the visual effect is heavy. Consider combining.
- **Suggested fix**: Move the first citation to the end of the second sentence (covering both the velocity statement and the $\sqrt{s} = 2m_\chi$ consequence) and cite both Cirelli and Pinetti at the end of the decay-energy sentence. This reduces four citations to two without losing provenance.

### Issue 6: "Spectral wedge" metaphor not standard

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 4
- **Quote**: "adding a spectral ``wedge'' near $E_\gamma \sim m_\chi$"
- **Problem**: The term "wedge" in double quotes is not standard terminology. It likely refers to the hard photon feature from direct $W$ radiation, but the shape is better described as a "shoulder" or "hard tail" in the literature. Using non-standard metaphorical language risks confusion.
- **Suggested fix**: Replace with: "adding a hard photon tail near $E_\gamma \sim m_\chi$."

### Issue 7: \textsc{Pythia} formatting

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 3
- **Quote**: `\textsc{Pythia}`
- **Problem**: The LaTeX command `\textsc` produces small caps, not the standard typesetting for software names. The Pythia documentation and most HEP papers use `\texttt{PYTHIA}` or simply `Pythia` in upright font. Small caps would render as "ᴘʏᴛʜɪᴀ".
- **Suggested fix**: Use `\\texttt{Pythia}` or simply `Pythia` (regular font).

### Issue 8: Missing mention of PPPC tables

- **Location**: Section 1.4.1, paragraph 3
- **Quote**: "The spectra $dN_\gamma^{(f)}/dE$ for each channel are computed using Monte Carlo event generators such as \textsc{Pythia} \cite{Cirelli:2010xx, Hooper:2024}"
- **Problem**: The `\cite{Cirelli:2010xx}` reference is the PPPC 4 DM ID paper, which provides pre-tabulated spectra widely used in Fermi-LAT analyses — but the text doesn't mention these tables by name. Since the PPPC tables are the actual tool used in every analysis in this thesis, naming them explicitly here creates a clean forward reference.
- **Suggested fix**: "...using Monte Carlo event generators such as Pythia, with the resulting spectra tabulated in standard libraries such as PPPC 4 DM ID \cite{Cirelli:2010xx}."
