## 1.2.4 Beyond Standard Freeze-out

The derivation in Section~\ref{sec:1.2.2} assumed a single stable particle annihilating through an $s$-wave, velocity-independent process with perturbative couplings. Realistic dark matter models often deviate from this minimal picture. Several physical effects can modify the relationship between the annihilation cross-section during freeze-out and the one relevant for indirect detection today. Because this thesis is concerned with extracting constraints on $\langle \sigma v_\mathrm{rel} \rangle$ from gamma-ray observations, we briefly survey the most important refinements.

**Velocity-dependent cross-sections.** In the non-relativistic regime, the annihilation cross-section admits an expansion in powers of the relative velocity \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz},
\begin{equation}
\sigma v_\mathrm{rel} \simeq \sigma_0 + \sigma_1\, v_\mathrm{rel}^2 + \mathcal{O}(v_\mathrm{rel}^4) \,,
\label{eq:partial_wave}
\end{equation}
where the $\sigma_0$ term corresponds to $s$-wave ($\ell = 0$) annihilation and the $v_\mathrm{rel}^2$ term to $p$-wave ($\ell = 1$). For $s$-wave processes, the cross-section is essentially constant from freeze-out ($v_\mathrm{rel} \sim 0.3\,c$) to the present-day galactic halo ($v_\mathrm{rel} \sim 10^{-3}\,c$). If the $s$-wave amplitude vanishes — as can happen due to symmetry arguments such as helicity suppression in Majorana fermion annihilation into light fermions — the leading contribution is $p$-wave and the present-day rate is suppressed by a factor of $v_\mathrm{rel}^2 \sim 10^{-6}$ relative to the freeze-out value \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}. For indirect detection, this suppression renders $p$-wave-dominated channels practically invisible. In the analyses presented in this thesis, we focus on $s$-wave annihilation channels, where the thermal relic cross-section $\langle \sigma v_\mathrm{rel} \rangle_\mathrm{cosmo}$ serves as a meaningful benchmark for indirect detection sensitivity.

**Sommerfeld enhancement.** When dark matter couples to a mediator with mass $M_V \ll M_\chi$, the resulting long-range force distorts the wave function of the incoming pair, enhancing the annihilation probability at low velocities \cite{Hisano:2004ds,Cirelli:2024ssz}. The enhancement factor $S$ can be computed from the Schrödinger equation for the two-body system. For an attractive Coulomb-like potential with effective coupling $\alpha_g$, the result is \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}
\begin{equation}
S = \frac{2\pi\alpha_g / v_\mathrm{rel}}{1 - e^{-2\pi\alpha_g / v_\mathrm{rel}}} \,,
\label{eq:sommerfeld}
\end{equation}
which grows as $S \propto 1/v_\mathrm{rel}$ for $v_\mathrm{rel} \ll \pi\alpha_g$ and reduces to $S \to 1$ in the perturbative regime $v_\mathrm{rel} \gg \pi\alpha_g$. For a massive mediator (Yukawa potential), the enhancement saturates at $v_\mathrm{rel} \sim M_V / M_\chi$ and can exhibit resonant peaks at specific mass ratios \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz,ArkaniHamed:2008qn}. The crucial consequence for indirect detection is that the Sommerfeld factor can differ substantially between the freeze-out epoch ($v_\mathrm{rel} \sim 0.2\,c$, where the enhancement is moderate) and the present-day halo ($v_\mathrm{rel} \sim 10^{-3}\,c$, where $S$ can reach $\mathcal{O}(10^2$--$10^3)$) \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}. A dark matter candidate that freezes out with the standard thermal cross-section can thus produce indirect detection signals enhanced by orders of magnitude, a possibility that has been invoked to explain anomalous positron measurements \cite{ArkaniHamed:2008qn}. Conversely, it means that the thermal relic cross-section is not always the correct target for indirect searches in Sommerfeld-enhanced models.

**Co-annihilation.** If the dark sector contains additional particles with masses $M' - M \lesssim \text{few} \times T_\mathrm{fo}$, these nearly degenerate states remain abundant during freeze-out and contribute to the depletion of the dark matter density \cite{Griest:1990kh,Cirelli:2024ssz}. The relic abundance is then governed by an effective cross-section that sums over all co-annihilating pairs, weighted by their relative equilibrium abundances \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz},
\begin{equation}
\langle \sigma_\mathrm{eff} v \rangle = \sum_{i,j} \langle \sigma_{ij} v_{ij} \rangle \, \frac{n_\mathrm{eq}^i \, n_\mathrm{eq}^j}{n_\mathrm{eq}^2} \,.
\label{eq:coann}
\end{equation}
If the heavier partners carry stronger interactions (e.g., color charge in supersymmetric scenarios), they can substantially increase $\sigma_\mathrm{eff}$ and reduce the relic density below what the lightest state alone would predict. For indirect detection, however, only the lightest (surviving) species annihilates in the galactic halo, typically with a smaller cross-section than $\sigma_\mathrm{eff}$. Co-annihilation can therefore weaken the connection between the observed relic abundance and the indirect detection signal.

**Resonant annihilation.** When dark matter annihilates through an $s$-channel mediator whose mass sits near $m_\mathrm{med} \approx 2 M_\chi$, the cross-section exhibits a Breit--Wigner resonance \cite{Griest:1990kh,Gondolo:1990dk}. The thermally averaged cross-section becomes sharply velocity-dependent: if the resonance lies slightly above $2M_\chi$, the annihilation rate can be enhanced at lower velocities (when the kinetic energy brings the center-of-mass energy onto the pole), potentially boosting the present-day signal relative to the freeze-out value \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}. Conversely, if the resonance is accessed during freeze-out but not at the lower halo velocities, the late-time signal can be suppressed. This effect introduces a strong model dependence in the relationship between the thermal relic cross-section and the indirect detection rate, a possibility we discuss further when presenting our upper limits.

These refinements do not invalidate the WIMP paradigm but they complicate the mapping between the cosmological cross-section and the astrophysical signal. In the standard $s$-wave scenario without Sommerfeld enhancement or co-annihilation, the freeze-out value $\langle \sigma v_\mathrm{rel} \rangle_\mathrm{cosmo} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-26}$ cm$^3$/s is also the value expected in the galactic halo, providing a clean experimental target. In more complex models, the relationship is modified in ways that are calculable but model-dependent. The analyses in this thesis adopt the standard thermal relic cross-section as the primary benchmark while noting where Sommerfeld-enhanced or velocity-dependent scenarios would shift the interpretation.
