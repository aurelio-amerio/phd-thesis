### Issue 1: Repetitive closing sentence on the CMB figure

- **Location**: Section 1.1.3, paragraph 2 (line 113)
- **Quote**: "The dramatic impact of cold dark matter on the CMB power spectrum is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:cmb}: the bottom panel compares the observed spectrum with that predicted in a universe without dark matter, showing how different the angular pattern would be without dark matter."
- **Problem**: This sentence repeats "without dark matter" twice and is essentially a restatement of what was already said two sentences earlier ("a universe without cold dark matter would produce a strongly suppressed third peak"). - **Suggested fix**: Delete or substantially shorten this sentence. The figure reference on line 111 already directs the reader to the figure. If you want a closing reference, something like: "Figure~\ref{fig:cmb} illustrates the contrast between the observed spectrum and the prediction for a baryon-only universe."

## Minor Issues (🟢)

### Issue 5: Slight redundancy between section intro and chapter intro

- **Location**: Section 1.1, opening paragraph (lines 5–8) vs. Chapter intro (1.0, line 6)
- **Problem**: The chapter introduction already says "Flat rotation curves in spiral galaxies, the dynamics and gravitational lensing of galaxy clusters, and the detailed pattern of temperature anisotropies in the Cosmic Microwave Background all point consistently toward a dominant, non-luminous matter component." The section opening largely restates this. Some overlap is expected between a chapter intro and section intro, but the near-identical framing could feel repetitive to a sequential reader.
- **Suggested fix**: Minor — consider varying the phrasing slightly in the section opener to emphasize a different angle (e.g., the independence of the probing techniques rather than the list of phenomena).

### Issue 6: "probably the best argument" — hedging calibration

- **Location**: Line 8
- **Quote**: "...is probably the best argument that dark matter is a physical reality..."
- **Problem**: "Probably" slightly undermines an otherwise confident claim. In the thesis voice, the author normally takes stronger positions and reserves hedging for genuinely uncertain quantities.
- **Suggested fix**: Consider "is arguably the strongest argument" or simply "is the strongest argument" — both are more assertive while remaining defensible.

### Issue 7: dSph mass-to-light ratios — citation clustering

- **Location**: Line 51
- **Quote**: "$M/L \sim 10\,M_\odot/L_\odot$ in the classical dwarfs, to several hundred in Draco ($M/L \sim 300$), to well over a thousand in ultra-faint systems like Segue~1~\cite{Walker:2009zp,Cirelli:2024ssz,Bullock:2017xww}"
- **Problem**: Three citations are clustered at the end of a compound statement with specific numerical values. It's unclear which source supports which number. Walker et al. is the primary observational reference, while Cirelli and Bullock are reviews.
- **Suggested fix**: Place `\cite{Walker:2009zp}` after the specific numerical claims and leave the reviews at the end, or use separate citations for the classical vs. ultra-faint numbers.

### Issue 8: SZ effect paragraph — "recall that $\Omega_b \approx 0.05$"

- **Location**: Line 72
- **Quote**: "(recall that $\Omega_b \approx 0.05$)"
- **Problem**: This is the first time $\Omega_b \approx 0.05$ appears numerically in the section. The "recall" is anticipatory — the reader hasn't been told this yet in Section 1.1 (it appears in the chapter intro as $\Omega_b h^2 \approx 0.022$, which is a different quantity). The parenthetical form "(recall that...)" is slightly informal for the thesis register.
- **Suggested fix**: Replace with a direct statement: "Since BBN and CMB measurements independently constrain the baryon density to $\Omega_b \approx 0.05$ (Section~\ref{sec:1.1.3}), SZ measurements of the cluster baryon fraction..." — but this creates a forward reference to the same section. Simpler: just state it as a fact without "recall": "(where $\Omega_b \approx 0.05$)".

### Issue 9: The BAO paragraph transitions abruptly from CMB context

- **Location**: Line 149
- **Problem**: The BAO paragraph begins "Baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) preserve the imprint of these primordial sound waves..." immediately after the LSS/structure formation discussion. The transition is adequate but slightly abrupt — the reader must mentally shift from "DM seeds structure formation" to "sound waves leave a different kind of imprint." A transitional sentence connecting the two would smooth the flow.
- **Suggested fix**: Consider opening with: "Beyond the overall shape of the matter power spectrum, the primordial sound waves leave a second, sharper imprint on the distribution of matter."

### Issue 10: "Hot baryonic gas" in Bullet Cluster caption

- **Location**: Line 90 (figure caption)
- **Quote**: "The colored map shows the X-ray image of the hot baryonic gas..."
- **Problem**: The map in typical Bullet Cluster figures from Clowe et al. uses a specific color scale. "Colored map" is vague. If the figure uses a pink/magenta color scheme (as is standard), stating the color explicitly would help the reader parse the image.
- **Suggested fix**: If applicable: "The pink/magenta shading shows the X-ray emission from the hot intracluster gas..."

### Issue 11: "figure from Cirelli et al." — credit formatting

- **Location**: Line 93
- **Quote**: "Adapted from Clowe et al.~\cite{Clowe:2006eq}, figure from Cirelli et al.~\cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}."
- **Problem**: The credit phrasing is slightly ambiguous — was the figure taken from the Cirelli review (which reproduced it from Clowe), or is it a different rendering? The thesis convention is `Credit: [source]`. "Adapted from" + "figure from" is a double attribution that could confuse.
- **Suggested fix**: Clarify: "Credit: Cirelli et al.~\cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}, after Clowe et al.~\cite{Clowe:2006eq}." or "Reproduced from Cirelli et al.~\cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}; original data from Clowe et al.~\cite{Clowe:2006eq}."
