## 1.2.3 Theoretical Bounds on the WIMP Mass

The relic abundance formula derived in the previous subsection, combined with basic physical requirements, brackets the viable WIMP mass range from both below and above. These bounds define the GeV--TeV search window that has guided the experimental program for decades.

**The Lee--Weinberg bound.** A lower limit on the mass of a thermal WIMP follows from requiring that the freeze-out relic density does not exceed the observed dark matter abundance. For a particle that annihilates via the Standard Model weak interaction in an $s$-wave process, the annihilation cross-section scales as $\sigma_0 \approx G_F^2 M^2 / 2\pi$, where $G_F = \sqrt{2}\,g_2^2 / 8M_W^2$ is the Fermi constant \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}. Since the relic abundance is inversely proportional to the cross-section (Eq.~\eqref{eq:relic_abundance}), lighter particles annihilate less efficiently and freeze out with a larger residual density. Inserting this estimate into the relic abundance formula and imposing $\Omega_\chi h^2 \lesssim 0.12$ yields \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz,Lee:1977ua}
\begin{equation}
M \gtrsim 3 \, \mathrm{GeV} \qquad \text{(Lee--Weinberg bound)} \,.
\label{eq:lee_weinberg}
\end{equation}
This bound applies specifically to particles that annihilate through the $W$ and $Z$ bosons of the Standard Model. Dark matter candidates that interact through alternative mediators — such as a dark photon or a new scalar — are not subject to this constraint and can be lighter, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:1.2.1} \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}.

**The unitarity bound.** An upper limit arises from requiring that the annihilation cross-section remains consistent with partial-wave unitarity. For a generic $s$-wave $2 \to 2$ annihilation process with coupling $g$, the cross-section scales as $\langle \sigma v_\mathrm{rel} \rangle \sim g^4 / 4\pi M^2$, which decreases with increasing mass. The maximum value of $g$ is bounded by perturbativity, $g^2 \lesssim 4\pi$, imposing a ceiling on the cross-section for any given mass \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz}. A particle that is too heavy will have a cross-section that falls below the thermal relic value $\langle \sigma v_\mathrm{rel} \rangle_\mathrm{cosmo}$, causing it to freeze out prematurely with an overabundant relic density. Requiring $\Omega_\chi h^2 \lesssim 0.12$ translates into the constraint \cite{Cirelli:2024ssz,Griest:1989wd}
\begin{equation}
M \lesssim 100 \, \mathrm{TeV} \qquad \text{(unitarity bound)} \,.
\label{eq:unitarity}
\end{equation}
An independent lower bound at $M \gtrsim 3$ MeV follows from requiring that the dark matter freezes out before the onset of BBN ($T \sim \mathrm{MeV}$), so that the additional energy density and annihilation products do not spoil the successful predictions for the primordial light element abundances \cite{Hooper:2018kfv}.

Together, the Lee--Weinberg and unitarity bounds define the mass window
\begin{equation}
3 \, \mathrm{GeV} \lesssim M_\chi \lesssim 100 \, \mathrm{TeV}
\label{eq:wimp_window}
\end{equation}
for WIMPs that annihilate through Standard Model weak interactions. The lower end of this range is accessible to direct detection experiments, while the upper end pushes against the reach of current ground-based gamma-ray telescopes. For indirect detection through annihilation products, the thermal relic cross-section $\langle \sigma v_\mathrm{rel} \rangle_\mathrm{cosmo} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-26} \, \mathrm{cm}^3/\mathrm{s}$ serves as a natural benchmark: any instrument capable of probing this value directly tests the thermal freeze-out hypothesis. As we will discuss in Chapter~\ref{ch:4}, the Fermi Large Area Telescope has reached this sensitivity for masses up to several tens of GeV, while the Cherenkov Telescope Array will extend the reach into the TeV domain.

The bounds derived here assume the simplest freeze-out scenario: a single stable particle annihilating through a two-body $s$-wave process with velocity-independent cross-section. Several physical effects can modify this picture and shift the effective mass range, as we discuss next.
