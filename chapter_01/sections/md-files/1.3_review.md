## Important Issues (yellow)

### Issue 1: "Hooper:2024" citing a textbook for primary numerical claims
- **Location**: 1.3.1, line 38; 1.3.3, lines 102–103, 109, 118, 119.
- **Quote (line 38)**: "...transferring a recoil energy $E_R \sim \mu_A^2 v^2 / m_A$, where $\mu_A = M_\chi m_A / (M_\chi + m_A)$ is the reduced mass of the system~\cite{Dodelson:2020bqr}."
- **Quote (line 119)**: "...excluding the thermal relic value for masses below ${\sim}\,100$~GeV in the $b\bar{b}$ channel~\cite{Fermi-LAT:2015att,Hooper:2024}."
- **Problem**: `Hooper:2024` is a textbook, and is being cited for several factual claims throughout 1.3.3 (the s-wave equivalence claim line 103, the dwarf-flux ratio line 118, the Fermi-LAT dSph bound line 119) and once in 1.3.1 (line 62). For the dwarf-flux ratio and the Fermi-LAT bound the primary `Fermi-LAT:2015att` is already attached, so `Hooper:2024` is a confirmatory reference and that is fine. The cluster citation on line 103 ("...providing a firm, parameter-free experimental target~\cite{Hooper:2024}") is the borderline case — the canonical primary reference for the s-wave thermal cross-section value is Steigman, Dasgupta & Beacom 2012 (`Steigman:2012nb` if present in the bibliography). The chapter_outline.md flags this as the canonical reference for 1.2.3, so it is presumably already in the bib.
- **Suggested fix**: Consider adding `Steigman:2012nb` (or whichever bib key was used in §1.2.2 for the 2.2e-26 cm^3/s benchmark) alongside `Hooper:2024` on line 103. This is a small upgrade in citation provenance, not a correction. Leave the rest of the textbook citations alone — they are appropriate for general-survey statements in a thesis introduction.

### Issue 2: Quantitative claim "$10^{-46}$ cm$^2$ at $M_\chi \sim 100$ GeV" is consistent with the data but lacks a citation
- **Location**: 1.3.1, line 53.
- **Quote**: "At higher masses ($M_\chi \sim 100$~GeV), the limits relax to ${\sim}\,10^{-46}$~cm$^2$ as the local number density of dark matter particles decreases."
- **Problem**: The numerical value is correct in order of magnitude (LZ 2024 reaches roughly $2 \times 10^{-47}$ cm$^2$ at the minimum and roughly $5 \times 10^{-47}$ cm$^2$ at 100 GeV; the relaxation pattern is universal across LZ/XENONnT/PandaX-4T), but the sentence sits at the end of a paragraph where the previous sentence (line 52) already cites `{LZ:2022lsv,XENON:2023cxc,PandaX-4T:2021bab}`. Strictly the citation on line 52 should cover the 100 GeV value as well, but the prose breaks the citation flow with the explanatory line 53 in between. A reader skimming for the source of the 100 GeV number will not find it attached to the sentence itself.
- **Suggested fix**: Optional. Either move the `\cite{LZ:2022lsv,XENON:2023cxc,PandaX-4T:2021bab}` to the end of line 53 instead of line 52 (so both numerical claims are covered), or add `LZ:2024zvo` to line 53 since that is the most recent xenon SI limit. No factual issue.

### Issue 3: "$g^2/(4\pi)^2 \sim 10^{-3}$" cross-section penalty estimate uses an unspecified $g$
- **Location**: 1.3.2, line 83.
- **Quote**: "...each paying a cross-section penalty of roughly $g^2/(4\pi)^2 \sim 10^{-3}$ for the additional emission..."
- **Problem**: $g$ here is left implicit. For mono-jet (gluon emission) the coupling is $g_s$ with $\alpha_s/\pi \sim 0.04$, giving a $\sim 4\%$ penalty, not $10^{-3}$. For mono-photon (QED) $\alpha/\pi \sim 0.002 \approx 10^{-3}$. For mono-$Z$ it is electroweak. The single number $10^{-3}$ is closer to the QED case but is being used as a generic estimate covering the entire mono-X family. A literal-minded reader (or a referee) might object. The order-of-magnitude statement is defensible if read as "between $10^{-3}$ and a few percent depending on the radiator" but a referee in a viva might press on this.
- **Suggested fix**: Soften slightly: "...each paying a cross-section penalty of order $\alpha/\pi$ (a few times $10^{-3}$ to a few percent depending on the radiator)..." — or just "of order $\alpha/\pi$" without the numerical follow-on. This avoids over-specifying a single value across a family of distinct couplings.

## Minor Issues (green)

### Issue 4: Long sentence on line 15 has a soft tautology
- **Quote**: "...the same dark matter--Standard Model coupling can yield very different cross-sections in the non-relativistic regime probed by direct and indirect detection and the relativistic regime probed at colliders --- sometimes suppressed by powers of the dark matter velocity or the momentum transfer ---..."
- **Note**: Reading three times: the "and" between "...and indirect detection and the relativistic regime..." is grammatically correct but momentarily ambiguous on the first pass. Consider inserting "by" after the first "regime probed by": "...the non-relativistic regime probed by direct and indirect detection and by the relativistic regime probed at colliders..." — or recast slightly. Optional polish.

### Issue 5: "essentially requiring thermal relic dark matter to be heavier than $M_h/2 \approx 63$~GeV"
- **Location**: line 85.
- **Note**: The bound on invisible Higgs BR constrains the Higgs-portal coupling, not directly the dark matter mass. The statement as written conflates "invisible decay is kinematically forbidden if $M_\chi > M_h/2$" with "current data require $M_\chi > M_h/2$ in Higgs-portal scenarios" — the second is approximately true given how strongly BR$_{inv} < 0.11$ constrains the portal coupling, but the wording "essentially requiring" overstates: a Higgs-portal coupling small enough to satisfy BR$_{inv} < 0.11$ while still producing the thermal relic abundance is technically possible at low masses (very tuned), and the bound is on the coupling, not on the mass. Optional rewording: "...essentially excluding Higgs-portal thermal relic dark matter below $M_h/2 \approx 63$~GeV except in fine-tuned regions..." Not a correction, just calibrated hedging.

## Out-of-Scope Suggestions (recorded but do not act on)

- **Asymmetric DM and freeze-in colliders.** The collider subsection focuses on s-channel mediators and invisible Higgs decays. A complete survey would also mention long-lived particle searches (HSCP, displaced vertices) which are a major LHC programme. These were deliberately excluded per the revision plan; the omission is principled.
- **Light DM accelerator experiments.** Sub-GeV DM at fixed-target experiments (LDMX, NA64) gets no mention. Out of scope for an introductory chapter framed around WIMPs and indirect-detection gamma-rays, but worth recording.
- **Axion ALPs limits from cosmology and gamma-ray observations.** The axion paragraph routes to Snowmass for haloscope searches but does not mention that gamma-ray indirect signatures of ALPs (axion-photon mixing in magnetic fields, spectral wiggles in blazar spectra) would actually appear in the thesis's own instrument. The author may want to forward-reference this to Chapter 8 or just leave it.
- **Antideuterons / antihelium.** Line 106 lists "heavier anti-nuclei" generically. The GAPS experiment specifically targets antideuterons and is a recent (2024–2025) commissioning milestone. Out of scope for §1.3 but a candidate for §1.4 multi-messenger.
- **Dark photons / hidden-sector benchmarks.** The collider subsection sticks to a mediator $Z'$ as the canonical example. Dark photon $A'$ benchmarks (FASER, LHCb dark photon search) are increasingly central to the LHC dark sector programme. Probably out of scope here; could be revisited in 1.4 multi-messenger.
