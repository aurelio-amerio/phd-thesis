Comments on chapter 4:
- section 4.1.4: You state "the same mass and channel that fit the spectrum also predict the correct spatial distribution, and the same halo profile that matches the morphology yields the observed flux level." --> I would argue that this is not correct. The particle physics properties are decoupled from the spatial profile entirely (talking about cold dark matter without self-interactions). First of all, you can accommodate any particle physics model for WIMPs with whatever spatial profile the GCE has. How you produce this spatial profile is a matter for astrophysicists to explain. You can only measure and fit the profile of the excess, it is an observation. Not a prediction.
- section 4.1.4: Where you say "The inferred cross section is degenerate with the assumed local dark matter density ...", I would argue that recent measurements of the local DM density even favour larger values > 0.4 GeV/cm^3, so the shift may be even larger.
- section 4.2.1: You say "By the late 2010s, the NPTF photon statistics, wavelet power, and stellar bulge morphology appeared to have settled the question in favor of the MSP interpretation. As we will see in Section 4.3, however, subsequent studies exposed weaknesses in each of these analyses, casting doubt on their conclusions and setting off a series of rebuttals and refinements." --> The paragraph before mentions the Bartels et al. paper of 2017 [215], which is very different from the other papers you mentioned earlier in that section. The NPTF works and Macias et al. all are based on templates with fixed spatial morphology while the Bartels paper is using skyFACT, i.e., they allow for a data-driven optimisation of the spatial morphologies (among other features). The caveats in Sec. 4.3 do not necessarily apply to skyFACT.
- section 4.2.2: Can you better explain the statement that "unlike the disk and globular cluster populations, which are continuously replenished with freshly recycled pulsars"? For the disc, I agree but I do not see how globular clusters as old objects would be able to replenish their MSP content other than via dynamical formation which should also apply to the Milky Way bulge or nuclear stellar cluster.
- section 4.2.2: What I was missing in section 4 is a clear description of why we should look into globular clusters at all and what they may have to do with the GCE. In the very end, during the conclusions, you name it: Disruption of globular clusters in the history of the Milky Way can deposit MSPs in the bulge and generate a bulge MSP population. This deposition mechanism should be mentioned in section 4 to better motivate the whole globular cluster story line.
- section 4.3: "both for and against the MSP hypothesis, was obtained through template-based analyses that share a common vulnerability" --> As said before, this is not really true for the Bartels et al. paper [215] which mitigates the issues you name in this section by construction (of course, there are other issues one could mention).
- section 4.3.2: You write "The presence of a power-law tail at higher energies is difficult to reconcile with a purely MSP origin". I do not see it this way and Manconi et al. [239] also state that such a tail would naturally occur in MSP populations embedded in reasonably strong photon fields due to inverse Compton emission. 
- section 4.3.3: To my taste, the emphasis on List el al. [221] is way too strong and not really supported by the results they present. They for sure have a point but when you look deeper into the methodology, I have my doubts about the robustness of all of their claims. First, everything in terms of astrophysics still relies on the same bad templates that have been shown multiple times to exhibit mis-modelled features. Second, in their appendix figure S12 (PRL manuscript), they show what happens to their dN/dF when a different Galactic diffuse model is used for the inference. The whole distribution shifts to larger fluxes compatible with previous results. To me, this indicates that whatever they do is super sensitive to background mis-modelling. Third, their simulator is such that all point-like sources are simulated with the same shared intrinsic gamma-ray spectrum. This is very unnatural for the Galactic centre and its many source classes. This is forceful mis-modelling of the physics and will impact the final outcome. --> For the thesis, you can build up the claim like this to make the point for dark matter. Up to you.
- section 4.5.2: Introduces MSP as acronym again.
- section 4.5.4: At the end of the section, you may also argue about the assumption that all GCs and their MSP populations share a common L_0, sigma_L. That needs to be verified or at least corrected by the average age due to energy losses of MSPs over time if they formed at different times in different clusters.
- section 4.5.5: You say that "Given systematic efforts that have been conducted to search for radio pulsations from unassociated Fermi sources, this possibility seems unlikely." --> Do you have a reference for these systematic searches? To my knowledge, there have been a few targeted observations in radio towards the Milky Way's bulge (with some detections) but I do not know of a systematic scan in radio.
- section 4.5.5: At the end where you discuss the impact of cluster variation effects, I find this to be the more realistic assessment since, to me, it would be a big surprise that 50+ globular clusters and their MSPs behave exactly the same. Just because of the energy loss argument with time, I would expect such variations.

Comments for potential consideration:

- section 1.4.5: Your narrative is very heavily leaning towards WIMPs. Maybe you want to add a small comment about the length scale/time scale at which this isotropisation happens since local information about charged cosmic rays are accessible to some extent.

- section 1.4.7: Talking about unresolved anomalie -- There comes to my mind another curiosity that has been discussed in the context of dark matter, namely the unusually high ionisation fraction in the Galactic centre (Pedro dLT has some papers on that linking it to DM).
- section 1.4.7: Overall, given that you write this as an intro to the thesis, I am missing a paragraph that mentions the higher energies in the context of WIMPs. While there is the GCE as an anomaly, there is the entire TeV range for WIMPs that current generation instruments can only marginally probe. In that regard, many minimal dark matter models can only be tested with CTAO leaving currently open this window at the TeV scale. Most of your work is targeting rather GeV gamma rays but this outlook on higher energies should be there to complete the overview.
- section 2.1.3: When discussing the uncertainties of the Galactic diffuse emission, you should also mention the issue of dark gas on small scales which you cannot infer with the typical techniques, neither with proxies like CO or directly from its emission/absorption lines.

- chapter 3 intro: I find the label "noise-dominate regimes" a bit misleading if it is not explained properly. It becomes more or less clear when you read the whole thesis but, a priori, when I read noise dominated, I think about analyses that are statistics limited with a weak signal and instrument-related noise. What you are dealing with is probably closer to systematics dominated (or background dominated) where you have enough statistics to do the analysis but you need to control the level of systematic uncertainty of each of the components. I suggest to explain this term "noise-dominated" right at the beginning of the intro to set the stage.
