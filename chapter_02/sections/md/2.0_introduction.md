The gamma-ray sky is rich and complex, dominated by diffuse emission that traces the interstellar gas of the Milky Way, punctuated by thousands of individual point sources, and underlaid by a faint, isotropic extragalactic glow. For indirect searches for dark matter, these astrophysical gamma rays constitute the primary background. Extracting a faint dark matter signal—whether from annihilation at the Galactic Center or from the collective emission of unresolved subhalos—requires us to understand where these background photons originate and how our instruments record them.

Standard analytical methods successfully classify the brightest objects and map the prominent diffuse components, but they struggle with overlapping populations and instrumental constraints such as finite spatial resolution and detection thresholds. These challenges obscure the faintest dark matter signatures and the broader population of unresolved sources. Distinctly isolating a dark matter signal from this background therefore requires novel statistical and machine learning techniques capable of modeling complex systematic uncertainties.

In this chapter we establish the astrophysical and instrumental framework that underpins the analyses in the remainder of this thesis. Section 2.1 reviews the fundamental production mechanisms of high-energy gamma rays, detailing the hadronic and leptonic processes that generate the Galactic foreground. Section 2.2 surveys the dominant source populations, progressing from Galactic entities such as millisecond pulsars to extragalactic classes like blazars, and formalizes the source-count distribution and its connection to the isotropic background. Finally, Section 2.3 describes the \textit{Fermi} Large Area Telescope, focusing on the instrumental response functions, detection thresholds, and data products that define the practical limits of current observations. The physical complexity and instrumental limitations introduced here constitute the primary systematic uncertainties that we address using statistical learning and simulation-based inference in the following chapters.
