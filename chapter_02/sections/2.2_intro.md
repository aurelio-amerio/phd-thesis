## 2.2 The Astrophysical Gamma-Ray Sky

The high-energy sky observed by the Fermi Large Area Telescope (Fermi-LAT) is a complex superposition of energetic processes spanning our local Galactic neighborhood and the broader universe. Phenomenologically, this emission falls into two domains. The Galactic sky is dominated by the diffuse foreground of cosmic-ray interactions and populated by energetic point sources, most notably pulsars. The extragalactic sky, in contrast, is characterized by an isotropic background arising primarily from unresolved active galactic nuclei and star-forming galaxies. A detailed understanding of the astrophysical processes behind these conventional emitters is necessary for dark matter indirect detection, where every ordinary astrophysical signal is a potential background. Isolating any dark matter contribution therefore requires a thorough characterization of the astrophysical gamma-ray sky.
