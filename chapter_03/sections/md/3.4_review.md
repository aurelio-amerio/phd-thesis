## Important Issues (🟡)

### Issue 1: Missing citation on consequences claim (3.4.1)
- **Location**: 3.4.1, final sentence
- **Quote**: "Any analysis that ignores this mismatch risks producing biased class prevalence estimates and, in the context of dark matter searches, unreliable upper bounds on the annihilation cross-section."
- **Problem**: This consequence claim is stated as general fact but lacks a citation. Paper 4 demonstrates this.
- **Fix**: Add `\cite{Amerio:2025sub}` at the end of this sentence.

### Issue 2: Overly long covariate shift paragraph (3.4.2)
- **Location**: 3.4.2, "Covariate shift" paragraph
- **Problem**: The paragraph covers six distinct ideas in one block (~12 lines), reducing readability.
- **Fix**: Split after the importance weighting sentence ("...corrected by importance weighting."). Start a new paragraph for the parametric approximation and sigmoid equation.

### Issue 3: "Genuine statistical challenge" phrasing (3.4.2)
- **Location**: 3.4.2, "Combined shift" paragraph
- **Quote**: "separating their effects is a genuine statistical challenge"
- **Problem**: "Genuine" has a mild promotional ring.
- **Fix**: Replace with "separating their effects is difficult."

### Issue 4: Abrupt transition to Application Preview (3.4.2)
- **Location**: 3.4.2, between "Combined shift" and "Application Preview"
- **Problem**: The combined shift paragraph ends with "Neither gives the correct answer when both effects are present." The next paragraph jumps to "we construct the first model..." without bridging.
- **Fix**: Add a bridging sentence at the start of the application preview: "Addressing this challenge requires a model that treats both effects simultaneously."

### Issue 5: Interpretive claim about faint-end depletion (3.4.2)
- **Location**: 3.4.2, covariate shift paragraph
- **Quote**: "depleting the faint end of the feature distribution"
- **Problem**: Slightly interpretive — the references say distributions differ, not specifically that the faint end is "depleted."
- **Fix**: Soften to "so the associated sample is biased toward brighter sources with well-localized counterparts."
