## Important Issues (🟡)

### Issue 1: Missing section introduction paragraph
- **Location**: Section 3.2, before 3.2.1
- **Problem**: The consolidated `section_3.2.md` has an introductory paragraph for the full section, but the individual subsection files (which will be the basis for the tex) do not. The existing tex for Sec. 3.1 includes an introductory paragraph under `\subsection{Frequentist and Bayesian Inference}` before the first `\subsubsection`. Section 3.2 should follow the same pattern.
- **Suggested fix**: Include the introductory paragraph from `section_3.2.md` (lines 4) in the final tex, placed between `\subsection` and the first `\subsubsection`.

### Issue 2: Inconsistent chapter cross-references
- **Location**: 3.2.1, paragraph 3: "Chapter~6"; 3.2.2, last paragraph: "Chapter~6"; 3.2.3, paragraph 4: "Chapter~6"
- **Problem**: The individual subsection `.md` files use hardcoded "Chapter~6", while the consolidated `section_3.2.md` correctly uses `Chapter~\ref{ch:paper1}`. The tex should use `\ref` commands throughout.
- **Suggested fix**: Replace all hardcoded chapter numbers with `\ref{ch:paper1}` (or similar label) in the tex output.

### Issue 3: Normalizing flow explanation could be tighter
- **Location**: 3.2.2, NPE paragraph
- **Quote**: "A normalizing flow maps samples from a simple base distribution (such as a multivariate Gaussian) through a sequence of parameterized, invertible transformations with tractable Jacobians, producing a flexible approximation to any target density"
- **Problem**: This is a good one-sentence description, but it anticipates Section 3.3 (ML in Astrophysics) content. Since normalizing flows are not used anywhere else in the thesis and NPE is re-introduced in Chapter 6, this level of detail may be unnecessary here. The outline says "Keep focus on NPE as the approach most directly relevant to the thesis" — which is done — but the normalizing flow detail is only needed in Ch. 6/9.
- **Suggested fix**: Keep but shorten to one clause: "typically implemented with normalizing flows~\cite{Papamakarios:2021}" — then redirect: "the mathematical details of these architectures are deferred to Chapters~6 and~9."

### Issue 4: Subtle AI pattern — "This is precisely the setting that..."
- **Location**: 3.2.1, last sentence of the dN/dS example paragraph
- **Quote**: "This is precisely the setting that simulation-based inference was designed to address"
- **Problem**: "This is precisely the setting" is a mild significance-inflation pattern. In an otherwise natural section, this stands out.
- **Suggested fix**: "This is the kind of problem that SBI addresses, and we describe..." or simply merge the transition with the next sentence.

### Issue 5: Mild AI pattern — three-part structure in 3.2.2 amortization paragraph
- **Location**: 3.2.2, paragraph beginning "All three strategies..."
- **Quote**: "running the simulator and training the neural network --- are performed once during an upfront training phase. After this phase, applying the trained surrogate to new observed data is nearly instantaneous"
- **Problem**: The paragraph follows an implicit three-step pattern and ends with "eliminating the need for hand-crafted summary statistics and preserving information that traditional methods would discard." This trailing participle clause is a mild -ing pattern per the humanizer.
- **Suggested fix**: Rephrase to: "...and the network preserves information that hand-crafted summary statistics would discard."

### Issue 6: Missing ABC citation
- **Location**: 3.2.2, first paragraph
- **Quote**: "Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC) developed in parallel as an entirely sampling-based alternative~\cite{Cranmer:2020}"
- **Problem**: Citing only Cranmer 2020 for ABC is indirect. ABC has foundational papers (e.g., Beaumont, Zhang & Balding 2002; Sisson, Fan & Tanaka 2007) or at minimum Marin et al. (2012) as a review. Even the outline lists `Cranmer:2020` for this, but a dedicated ABC reference would strengthen the citation quality.
- **Suggested fix**: Add at least one direct ABC reference alongside Cranmer:2020, or note this is introduced "as reviewed in~\cite{Cranmer:2020}" to make the indirect citation explicit.

### Issue 7: Paper 1 description slightly oversimplified
- **Location**: 3.2.2, last paragraph
- **Quote**: "The CNN outputs both a point estimate and a learned error bar for each flux bin, combining the roles of posterior mean and variance estimation."
- **Problem**: This phrasing suggests the CNN directly performs NPE (learning the full posterior). In reality, the CNN uses a heteroscedastic Gaussian loss to learn mean + variance for each bin — this is closer to amortized point estimation with learned aleatoric uncertainty than to full NPE with normalizing flows. The section intro calls this "NPE" but the CNN does not use normalizing flows; it uses a direct regression output. This nuance should be acknowledged to avoid scientific inaccuracy.
- **Suggested fix**: Clarify that the Paper 1 CNN performs amortized parameter estimation in the spirit of NPE — directly mapping observations to posterior summaries (mean and variance) — rather than learning the full posterior density via normalizing flows. This distinction is important for readers familiar with the SBI literature.

## Minor Issues (🟢)

### Issue 8: Em dash density
- **Location**: Throughout
- **Problem**: The section uses `---` (em dashes) frequently: "historically been called...although", "particle transport...formation of large-scale structure", "the term...is now preferred". While individually fine, the density is slightly elevated compared to Section 3.1's tex.
- **Suggested fix**: Convert 2–3 em dashes to commas or parenthetical constructions during tex conversion.

### Issue 9: "We now briefly describe" phrasing
- **Location**: 3.2.2, end of first paragraph
- **Quote**: "We now briefly describe the three main neural strategies that have emerged"
- **Problem**: Meta-narration ("we now describe") is acceptable in thesis writing but slightly formulaic.
- **Suggested fix**: Drop "We now briefly" → "Three main neural strategies have emerged, distinguished by..."

### Issue 10: "The computationally expensive steps" redundancy
- **Location**: 3.2.2, amortization paragraph
- **Problem**: The sentence explains amortization but partially restates what the previous paragraphs already described.
- **Suggested fix**: Tighten to one sentence: "All three strategies provide amortized inference: after the upfront cost of simulation and training, posterior evaluation for new data is nearly instantaneous."

### Issue 11: Inconsistent label format
- **Location**: All subsection labels use `\label{sec:3.2.X}` — consistent with 3.1. ✅ No issue.

### Issue 12: SBC acronym should be expanded at first use
- **Location**: 3.2.3, paragraph 2
- **Problem**: "SBC" is expanded as "simulation-based calibration (SBC)" correctly. ✅ No issue — this is fine.

### Issue 13: "Taken together" closing
- **Location**: 3.2.3, last paragraph
- **Quote**: "Taken together, SBC, coverage diagnostics, and problem-specific cross-checks form the validation toolkit..."
- **Problem**: "Taken together" is mildly formulaic. The closing sentence also follows a rule-of-three pattern.
- **Suggested fix**: "These three validation strategies — calibration, coverage, and problem-specific diagnostics — are applied in each of the analyses presented in subsequent chapters."
