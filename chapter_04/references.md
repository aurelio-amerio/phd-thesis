# Chapter 04: The Galactic Center Excess (GCE) - References & Sources

## 1. Reviews & Textbooks
*General consensus, theoretical foundations, and state-of-the-art summaries.*

### Murgia (2020) - "The Galactic Center Excess"
*   **Source:** *Annual Review of Nuclear and Particle Science (Vol. 70)* [**Review**]
    *   **Relevance:** A comprehensive review detailing the properties of the GCE (spectrum peaking at GeV, spherical morphology). It discusses the two primary competing explanations: Dark Matter annihilation vs. Millisecond Pulsars, providing a balanced state-of-the-art summary.

### Cirelli et al. (2024) - "Review of Particle Physics"
*   **Source:** *Particle Data Group (PDG)*
    *   **Relevance:** The standard reference in the field. The section "The $\gamma$ excess from the Galactic Center" chronicles the history of the signal from discovery to current debates, covering background model systematics and astrophysical alternatives.

### Hooper (2009) - "Particle Cosmology and Astrophysics"
*   **Source:** *Princeton University Press* [**Textbook**]
    *   **Relevance:** Provides the foundational concepts required to research the GCE, bridging particle physics and astronomy. It specifically covers dark matter halos, high-energy astrophysics, and the detection methods relevant to the signal.

## 2. Key Specific Papers
*Primary sources for specific claims, historical limits, and experimental results.*

### Goodenough & Hooper (2009) - "Possible Evidence For Dark Matter Annihilation In The Inner Milky Way From The Fermi Gamma Ray Space Telescope"
*   **Source:** *arXiv:0910.2998*
    *   **Relevance:** The seminal paper reporting the first evidence of the Galactic Center Excess. It identified a signal consistent with Dark Matter annihilation in the inner Galaxy, sparking the decade-long debate.

### Daylan et al. (2016) - "The Characterization of the Gamma-Ray Signal from the Central Milky Way: A Case for Annihilating Dark Matter"
*   **Source:** *arXiv:1402.6703*
    *   **Relevance:** The definitive characterization of the GCE morphology and spectrum. It established the signal's robust preference for a spherical, NFW-like profile and a spectrum consistent with ~30-40 GeV WIMPs annihilating to $b\bar{b}$.

### Abazajian et al. (2014) - "Resilience of the Bright $\gamma$-ray Excess in the Galactic Center to Interstellar Emission Modeling"
*   **Source:** *arXiv:1402.4090*
    *   **Relevance:** A rigorous analysis demonstrating that the excess persists across various background models, solidifying its existence as a physical signal rather than a modeling artifact.

## 3. References Breakdown by Section
*Detailed mapping of which sections to read for each thesis part.*

### Section 4.1: The Signal
**Topics:** Morphology, Spectrum, Robustness

*   **Daylan et al. (2016) [arXiv:1402.6703]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the detailed morphological analysis (NFW $\gamma \sim 1.25$) and spectral fits ($b\bar{b}$ channel). The primary reference for the "Dark Matter interpretation" parameters.
*   **Calore et al. (2015) [arXiv:1409.0042]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the extraction of the GCE spectrum with systematic uncertainties ("Calore spectra"). It defines the systematic error bars used in most subsequent analyses.
*   **Goodenough & Hooper (2009) [arXiv:0910.2998]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the historical context of the discovery and early characterization.

### Section 4.2: Interpretations & The Great Debate
**Topics:** DM vs. MSPs, NPTF, Hydrodynamic templates, Systematics

#### The Case for Millisecond Pulsars (Against DM)
*   **Bartels et al. (2016) [arXiv:1506.05104] & Lee et al. (2016) [arXiv:1506.05124]:**
    *   **Relevance:** The "NPTF" papers. Read for the Non-Poissonian Template Fitting method which provided the first strong statistical evidence that the GCE is composed of unresolved point sources (MSPs) rather than smooth DM emission.
*   **Macias et al. (2018) [arXiv:1611.06644] & Bartels et al. (2018) [arXiv:1711.04778]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the arguments that the GCE morphology follows the Stellar Kernel (mass distribution) rather than a DM NFW profile. These papers suggest the excess traces the central stellar bulge, strongly favoring an astrophysical origin (MSPs/XRBs).
*   **Abazajian (2011) [arXiv:1011.4275]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the early arguments proposing that a population of Millisecond Pulsars could naturally explain the excess without invoking new physics.

#### The "Revival" of the Dark Matter Hypothesis (Systematics)
*   **Leane & Slatyer (2019) [arXiv:1904.08430]:** *"Dark Matter Strikes Back"*
    *   **Relevance:** **Crucial Paper.** Read for the demonstration that the NPTF method can mistakenly attribute a smooth DM signal to point sources (spurious point sources) when the background model is imperfect, casting doubt on the robust MSP exclusion of DM.
*   **Leane & Slatyer (2020) [arXiv:2002.12370]:** *"The Enigmatic Galactic Center Excess: Spurious Point Sources and the Fermi Bubbles"*
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the analysis showing that unmodeled asymmetry in the Fermi Bubbles can mimic the statistical signature of point sources, further weakening the MSP argument.
*   **Cholis et al. (2022) [arXiv:2112.09706]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for a modern re-analysis using the most flexible background models to date. They find that when systematics are fully marginalized, the preference for point sources disappears, and the data is again robustly consistent with Dark Matter.
*   **Di Mauro (2021) [arXiv:2101.04694]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for a comprehensive study of heavy DM candidates using updated diffusion models, reinforcing the viability of the DM interpretation under modern constraints.

### Section 4.3: Stellar Clusters as Laboratories
**Topics:** Globular Clusters, MSP Luminosity Function, GCE constraints

*   **Amerio et al. (2025) [arXiv:2412.05220]:**
    *   **Relevance:** (Paper 3 of this Thesis) Read for the latest constraints using 15.8 years of Fermi-LAT data. It places upper limits on the MSP luminosity function, arguing that a GCE pulsar population would need to be anomalously faint.
*   **Hooper & Linden (2016) [arXiv:1606.09250]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the initial method of using Globular Clusters to constrain the field MSP population. The precursor to the analysis in Chapter 4/Paper 3.
*   **Cholis, Hooper & Linden (2014) [arXiv:1407.5583]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for the derivation of the gamma-ray luminosity function of MSPs from 47 Tucanae and field MSPs, highlighting the tension with the GCE requirements.
*   **Feng et al. (2024) [arXiv:2310.15859]:**
    *   **Relevance:** Read for a complementary study on MSP populations in 157 globular clusters and their dynamical evolution, discussing the tidal disruption scenario.
