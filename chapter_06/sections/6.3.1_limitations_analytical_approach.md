### 6.3.1 Limitations of the Analytical Approach
\label{sec:6.3.1}

The 1pPDF framework described in the previous section has produced the best available measurements of the sub-threshold $dN/dS$.  However, the generating-function formalism on which it rests imposes several simplifying assumptions that constrain both the physics it can capture and the practical efficiency of the analysis.

The first, and most fundamental, simplification concerns the treatment of spatial correlations.  The generating-function likelihood evaluates the photon-count probability pixel by pixel, treating each pixel as statistically independent \cite{Malyshev:2011zi, Zechlin:2015wdz}.  In reality, the Fermi-LAT point spread function spreads photons from a single source across multiple neighbouring pixels.  These inter-pixel correlations carry information about source brightness and morphology.  Because the 1pPDF formalism does not model them, it cannot fully exploit the spatial structure of the map — information that is, in principle, available and informative about the underlying source population.

A second limitation is the handling of energy information.  The original analyses of Malyshev \& Hogg and Zechlin et al. operated in a single broad energy band, typically 1--10~GeV \cite{Malyshev:2011zi, Zechlin:2015wdz}.  Subsequent extensions by Zechlin et al. and Lisanti et al. measured $dN/dS$ in several independent energy bands spanning 1--171~GeV \cite{Zechlin:2016yfe, Lisanti:2016jub}, but each band was analysed separately.  This approach recovers the energy dependence of the source-count distribution, yet it does not exploit correlations across energy bands — correlations that could, for example, help disentangle source populations with distinct spectra.

Third, the analytical method assumes a parametric functional form for $dN/dS$, most commonly a broken power law with a fixed number of breaks \cite{Zechlin:2015wdz}.  While this is a well-motivated and flexible choice for the smooth, single-population distributions measured so far, it may become limiting if the true $dN/dS$ contains features that do not conform to a piecewise power-law shape — for instance, bumps or cut-offs associated with distinct sub-populations at specific flux levels.  The community has recognised some of these issues: Zechlin et al. developed a Compound Poisson Generator formalism \cite{Zechlin:2015CPG} to address potential biases in the 1pPDF approach, though this alternative still operates within the generating-function framework.

Finally, the 1pPDF method is computationally demanding and not amortised.  The evaluation of the generating-function likelihood, especially in its pixel-dependent version \cite{Zechlin:2015wdz}, requires numerically intensive calculations that must be fully re-converged from scratch whenever a new dataset or a different parameter configuration is analysed \cite{Amerio:2023uet}.  This stands in contrast to machine learning approaches where, once the model is trained, inference on new data reduces to a fast forward pass.

These four limitations — neglected PSF correlations, independent treatment of energy bands, parametric assumptions on $dN/dS$, and the computational cost of non-amortised likelihood evaluation — motivate the search for an alternative approach.  The present analysis uses a single 1--10~GeV energy band, matching the baseline configuration of Zechlin et al. \cite{Zechlin:2015wdz}, so as to enable a direct comparison between the CNN result and the established 1pPDF benchmark.  However, the CNN framework naturally accommodates multi-band inputs and non-parametric output, as we describe in the following subsection.
