## 🔴 Critical Issues

### Issue 1: 6.0 and 6.1.1 say the same thing twice

This is the most serious problem. Compare the opening paragraphs:

> **6.0, ¶1**: "Part II examined dark matter annihilation signatures in specific, resolved astrophysical structures. Both strategies we explored encountered fundamental limitations. The Galactic Center presents the strongest expected signal, but it remains inextricably entangled with complex diffuse foregrounds..."

> **6.1.1, ¶1–3**: "Part II of this thesis investigated dark matter annihilation through two complementary approaches that both targeted individual, resolved structures. The first approach focused on the Galactic Center (Chapter~4). While this region offers the strongest expected dark matter signal..."

These are paraphrases of each other. A reader going from 6.0 into 6.1.1 would immediately feel they are re-reading the same material. The chapter introduction (6.0) is supposed to be a *high-altitude overview* that funnels into the chapter roadmap. Section 6.1.1 is supposed to *develop* the argument in detail — with specific cross-references, specific failures, and specific numbers. Right now, both operate at the same level of detail.

**Recommendation**: Rewrite 6.1.1 from scratch. The new version should:
- Not restate that Part II had two approaches (the reader already knows this from 6.0)
- Instead, *deepen* the argument: provide the specific physics reasons each approach hit a wall, with cross-references to the exact sections in Chapters 4–5 where these limitations were established
- Include specific quantitative anchors (e.g., the GCE's $\sim$15-year timeline, the 2428 unassociated sources, the TS > 25 threshold)
- Keep the concluding "common thread" paragraph, which is good

### Issue 2: 6.0 missing cross-references to Ch. 3 and Ch. 2

The outline explicitly lists: "**Cross-references:** Ch. 4 (GCE); Ch. 5 (subhalo searches); Ch. 3 §3.2 (SBI paradigm); Ch. 2 §2.2 (astrophysical source populations)."

The current 6.0 mentions SBI via CNNs and the source-count distribution, but never cross-references Ch. 3 §3.2 or Ch. 2 §2.2. These are important because they tell the reader where the methodological and observational foundations were laid.

**Recommendation**: In the roadmap paragraph (¶3), add parenthetical cross-references. E.g., "...introduces simulation-based inference (cf. Section~\ref{sec:sbi_paradigm}) via convolutional neural networks..."

## 🟡 Important Issues

### Issue 3: 6.0 ¶2 has an AI-tell structure

> "When identifying individual targets becomes intractable, a methodological shift is necessary."

This sentence uses the passive "is necessary" construction that reads like a textbook pronouncement rather than a scholarly argument. Compare the Chapter 5 opening:

> "The preceding chapter examined the Galactic Center Excess... That analysis confronted a difficulty... We now turn to a complementary search strategy..."

Chapter 5 uses active "we" constructions and directly connects chapters. Section 6.0 should do the same. The thesis style profile says: *Active "we" for own work*.

**Recommendation**: Rewrite the transition between ¶1 and ¶2 using active voice. E.g., "These limitations motivate a shift in strategy. We move from asking whether any specific source is a dark matter subhalo to a broader question: can we recover..."

### Issue 4: 6.1.1 closing paragraph is too vague

> "Both strategies fail when the gap between the detector threshold and the true source population is too large. When the signal is weak and the background is complex, searching for individual sources reaches a point of diminishing returns."

This is generic — it could describe any detection problem in any field. The outline's "common thread" directive says specifically: "individual-source methods rely on *identifying* a specific target or *classifying* individual sources. Both strategies are fundamentally constrained by the gap between the detector threshold and the true source population." The current draft captures the words but not the physics. The key insight is that the *interesting* population lives below the threshold, and source-by-source methods have zero reach there.

**Recommendation**: Sharpen the closing by making the physics explicit. The faint sources exist — we know this from the UGRB — but they are individually invisible. The problem is not that the methods "fail" but that they are structurally blind to the sub-threshold regime.

### Issue 5: 6.1.2 ¶3 is somewhat mechanical

> "Chapters~6 and 7 focus on characterizing the general unresolved source population. This population primarily consists of astrophysical objects, such as blazars, millisecond pulsars, and star-forming galaxies. The methodology developed in these chapters is designed to characterize these conventional astrophysical sources."

Three consecutive sentences all say roughly the same thing: "we study the astrophysical population." The outline's directive for this paragraph is more nuanced — it's a *scope statement* that simultaneously tells the reader what Part III does and what it does *not* do (DM → Ch. 8). The current version lists source classes without explaining why the scope restriction matters.

**Recommendation**: Compress to two sentences. State the scope and the reason: the astrophysical population must be characterized *first* before any DM component can be meaningfully isolated; the DM search requires a different technique (cross-correlations, Ch. 8).

## 🟢 Minor Issues

### Issue 6: 6.0 ¶4 "two main functions" is slightly promotional

"Characterizing the unresolved gamma-ray sky has two main functions in this thesis." — While factually correct, "two main functions" has a mildly salesy feel. Consider: "The results of this chapter feed into two downstream analyses."

### Issue 7: Tense inconsistency in 6.1.1

"our analysis found no statistically significant dark matter contribution" (past) immediately followed by "The attempt to identify individual subhalos is constrained" (present). Both discuss the same completed work and should use the same tense (past).

### Issue 8: "formidable" in 6.0

"the formidable look-elsewhere effect" — "formidable" is slightly inflated. The look-elsewhere effect is well-understood; the problem is its magnitude in this context, not that it is formidable. Consider simply: "the large look-elsewhere effect" or "the severe look-elsewhere effect."
