### 6.3.3 Inference on Spherical Data
\label{sec:6.3.3}

Fermi-LAT data are naturally represented as HEALPix maps on the sphere, where each pixel subtends an equal solid angle.  Standard planar CNNs cannot be applied directly to such maps without a projection step, yet naive projections of the sphere onto a flat plane introduce geometric distortions that may bias the network's learned features.  Fully spherical CNN architectures have been developed to address this problem \cite{Cohen:2018spherical, Perraudin:2019deepsphere, Krachmalnicoff:2019nnhealpix}, but they are generally slower than their planar counterparts and remain less mature in terms of available pre-trained architectures and optimisation infrastructure.

This work introduces an intermediate strategy, called \texttt{map2patches}, that retains the distortion-free properties of the HEALPix grid while exploiting the speed of standard planar convolutions \cite{Amerio:2023uet}.  The idea exploits the hierarchical structure of HEALPix: at its coarsest resolution ($N_\mathrm{side} = 1$), the sphere is partitioned into 12 equal-area base pixels.  Working at $N_\mathrm{side} = 64$, the \texttt{map2patches} algorithm subdivides the 49\,152-pixel map into 12 patches of 4\,096 pixels each, where each patch corresponds to one of the base pixels and can be mapped to a flat two-dimensional image.  Because this subdivision is a direct partitioning of the pixelisation grid rather than a re-sampling, it preserves both the area and the photon content of every pixel exactly, avoiding the interpolation artifacts that accompany standard Euclidean projections.

The 12 patches are processed in parallel by stacking them along a third dimension and applying 3D convolutions with filters of size $(N, N, 1)$.  The unit stride along the patch axis ensures that the network treats each patch independently during convolution, effectively applying standard 2D filters across the full sky without mixing border information between patches.  This tensor structure maps naturally onto highly optimised GPU and TPU convolution kernels, and it scales to multi-channel inputs — additional energy bands, for example, can be stacked as extra channels alongside the patch dimension.  The backbone architecture is EfficientNet~V2M \cite{Tan:2021efficientnetv2}, chosen for its favourable trade-off between expressiveness and memory footprint, the latter being particularly important for training with large batch sizes on accelerator hardware.

A partial trade-off of the \texttt{map2patches} strategy is the loss of information at patch boundaries and at angular scales larger than the patch size.  For the present analysis, this trade-off is acceptable: the features of interest — small-scale photon-count fluctuations from statistically isotropic point sources — are well contained within individual patches.  As a cross-check, a fully spherical CNN based on the NNHealpix architecture \cite{Krachmalnicoff:2019nnhealpix} was also implemented and applied to the same data.  The two approaches produce fully compatible $dN/dS$ reconstructions and error estimates, confirming that the patch-boundary effects are negligible for this analysis.  The \texttt{map2patches} implementation, however, achieves a speed-up of more than a factor of ten over the fully spherical approach for the entire training and validation pipeline \cite{Amerio:2023uet}.  Full details of the architecture, training procedure, and spherical cross-check are described in the remainder of this chapter (\S\ref{sec:6.7}).
