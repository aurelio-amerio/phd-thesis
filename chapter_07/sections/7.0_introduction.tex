The previous chapter demonstrated that the source-count distribution of faint gamma-ray emitters can be recovered from the photon-count statistics of the \textit{Fermi}-LAT sky using simulation-based inference and deep learning.
% The resulting $dN/dS$ extends as $\sim S^{-2}$ down to fluxes roughly 50 times below the nominal detection threshold, capturing a population of sub-threshold point sources --- blazars, star-forming galaxies, misaligned active galactic nuclei --- that contribute to the isotropic diffuse emission but remain individually undetected.
\blue{The resulting $dN/dS$ extends as $\sim S^{-2}$ well below the nominal detection threshold, capturing a population of sub-threshold point sources --- blazars, star-forming galaxies, misaligned active galactic nuclei --- that contribute to the isotropic diffuse emission but remain individually undetected.}
Yet the $dN/dS$ is a one-dimensional flux distribution: it tells us \emph{how many} sources exist at each flux, but it says nothing about \emph{where} any specific source is located.
The challenge, then, is whether this population-level statistical knowledge can be turned into spatial information.

This chapter takes the next step by complementing the source-count distribution with positional identifications.
% The core idea is to use the recovered $dN/dS$ as a generative model for ensembles of synthetic skies, enabling a frequentist statistical comparison that identifies candidate source directions below the detection threshold.
\blue{The core idea is to turn the recovered $dN/dS$ into spatial information through a frequentist comparison of the real sky against ensembles of synthetic skies, flagging candidate source directions below the detection threshold.}

Section~\ref{sec:threshold_limits} examines why the standard fixed-threshold cataloging paradigm discards useful information and produces a spatially inconsistent detection scale.
Section~\ref{sec:pop_to_spatial} describes the conceptual framework for converting the $dN/dS$ into spatial source candidates through simulated-sky comparison, and introduces the frequentist meta-parameters that control the depth and purity of the resulting catalog.
The remainder of the chapter presents the original research paper, which applies this framework to fourteen years of \textit{Fermi}-LAT data and delivers a publicly available probabilistic catalog.
Together with the $dN/dS$ of Chapter~\ref{ch:6}, this probabilistic catalog completes the characterization of the astrophysical point-source foreground against which any residual dark matter contribution must be identified, as pursued in Chapter~\ref{ch:8}.
